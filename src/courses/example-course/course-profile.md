# Course profile — Introduction to Statistics

An example course profile, filled in. Delete this folder once you have set
up your own courses, or keep it as a model.

## Course

- Name: Introduction to Statistics
- Code/number: STAT 101
- Language: English
- Level: intro
- Term length: 14 weeks
- Meeting pattern: 2 sessions per week, 75 minutes each

## Audience

- Typical background: comfortable with arithmetic and basic algebra; most
  have never interpreted a data set on their own
- Prerequisites: none
- Class size: 60

## The gap

Students most often struggle with sampling variability: they read a
single sample as the whole story. The artifacts aim at that gap.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students can:

1. Summarize a raw data set with an appropriate table, chart, and measure
   of center or spread, and say in plain words what the summary claims.
2. Construct and interpret a confidence interval, and explain what it does
   and does not claim.
3. Carry out a basic hypothesis test and state the conclusion, its
   assumptions, and its limits.
4. Distinguish correlation from causation in a claim taken from everyday
   media, and name what evidence would be needed to settle it.
5. Produce a short data-analysis report whose numbers, charts, and
   sentences agree with each other.

## Assessment mix

Weekly quizzes 20% (low stakes, retriable), four labs 30%, a mid-term 20%,
and a final data-analysis report 30%. Ki does not grade; this section
plans the shape the artifacts must serve.

## Tone and voice

Plain and encouraging. Everyday examples (food prices, sports, weather)
before abstract notation. Notation is introduced only after the idea it
records.

## Policies

Late work: accepted up to one week, with a stated cost. Collaboration on
labs in pairs; quizzes alone. AI use: allowed for practice, not on graded
submissions without attribution. Accessibility: materials are text-first
and screen-reader friendly; color never carries meaning alone.
