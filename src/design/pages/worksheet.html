Sampling variability — practice

Introduction to Statistics · STAT 101 · Week 4 · Serves outcomes 1 and 2 · 3 exercises · 35 minutes

Work in pairs. The first exercise walks you through the steps; the second leaves the middle to you; the third is yours alone. Write your answers where the lines are, and keep your rough work beside them.

Exercises

1

Ten delivery times from a takeaway: 22, 35, 26, 19, 41, 28, 24, 30, 27, 33 minutes.

  1. Find the mean.
  2. Work out the sample standard deviation.
  3. Build the 95% confidence interval for the mean.
  4. Write one sentence saying what the interval claims.

2

Your classmate says: “The 95% interval means there is a 95% chance the true mean is inside it.”

  1. Write where their sentence slips.
  2. Rewrite it so it stays true.

3

A survey of 200 students gives a mean study time of 7.5 hours a week, 95% CI 6.8 to 8.2. Another survey of 50 students gives 7.5 hours, 95% CI 5.9 to 9.1. Same mean, different intervals.

  1. Which interval would you put in a report, and why?
  2. Say what a reader should take from the wider one.

Instructor notes

Not for distribution. Exercise 1: answers near a mean of 28.5, sd around 6.5, interval roughly 24 to 33. Exercise 2: the slip is putting the 95% on the specific interval; a true rewrite names the procedure (“intervals built this way capture the true mean in 95% of samples”). Exercise 3: watch for students who pick the narrower interval without asking how the sample was drawn — the wide one is not worse data, it is fewer students.
